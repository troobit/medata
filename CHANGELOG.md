The requirements gained an operational region definition after review found the proposed flood fill seeds at the **frame centre** — a Nutrition5k rig assumption, where the *plate* is centred, against handheld capture where the user centres the *food*, so the fill could grow the bread's own top surface and collapse volume to zero on exactly the capture the feature exists to fix. β_c application was inverted to fail closed (the draft rejected artefacts recording a *different* reference, so artefacts recording *none* — i.e. every artefact that exists — would have been applied); the segmentation-accuracy Non-Goal was struck (the food mask now selects the geometric reference, so mask error changes the answer 3x); and the diagnostic became the **contact-ring median signed height**, which has a physical zero and was already computed by hand during diagnosis (+18…+26 mm on the table plane), replacing a next-strongest-consensus-plane measure that was scene-dependent, non-monotone, sign-ambiguous and collided with `consensusPolishMaxPasses`. The design settled on multi-plane extraction over region growing after the two objections against it failed on inspection: the 1920x1440 OOM was `svdFull(3xn)`'s O(n²) allocation, fixed at the root by the scatter-matrix form so memory no longer scales with points, and the testability objection did not hold at all — all candidates are equally deterministic pure functions and equally replayable. Design review then found four blockers, each verified in source: `restrictedResidualMaxMm` **cannot fire** (the residual is computed over `polishedInliers`, each within the 5 mm band, so RMS is ≤ 5 mm by construction — the justifying 13 mm figure was imported from the rejected flood-fill architecture); `depthIntrinsics` is written as all zeros by `ARKitCaptureEngine` so native-grid back-projection must derive them from colour including half-pixel terms; `maxIterations = 256` was sized for the dominant plane and has a 23 % chance of finding a 10 %-inlier minority plane; and guards applied after selection would discard an admissible plate plane when a phantom rim-ramp candidate won and failed. The design now bounds candidates to the food's neighbourhood, scores by largest connected inlier component (CC-RANSAC, whose documented target is a plane straddling two surfaces separated by a step — a plate rim), selects on ring support fraction rather than `|median|` (which has a 50 % cliff where the *table* plane reads ≈ 0 and is persisted with a perfect-looking diagnostic), and restores Decision 5's restricted-first ordering with a lazy edge-band fallback. `estimation-diagnostics.md` records why every existing diagnostic read healthy on the wrong plane: the residual is bounded by the inlier band and scored the wrong plane *better* (1.95 mm against 2.27 mm), inlier counts are inflated ~56x by colour-grid enumeration over a 256x192 depth map, and RANSAC selects on area — the worktop took 510,499 votes to the plate's ~64,000. The rimmed-plate case is stated as unresolved: a ring 8–25 mm outside the food can land on the rim rather than the well and clip food height, which is the inversion the bowl harm-ordering assumes cannot happen.

- **Adaptive LibreLinkUp poll interval** (`MedataCore/Sources/GlucoseIngestion/LibreLinkUpGlucoseSource.swift`, `MedataCore/Tests/GlucoseIngestionTests/LibreLinkUpPollIntervalTests.swift`, `specs/data/cgm-connect/`): the poll interval is now chosen after each successful fetch by a pure function of the readings it returned — **5 minutes** when the newest reading is below **5.0 mmol/L** or the fetch is falling at or faster than **0.111 mmol/L per minute** (`TrendsMath.mediumRateThreshold`), the **15-minute** baseline otherwise. Prompted by a field event on 2026-08-05: the Abbott app alarmed on a low while MeData displayed **4.2 mmol/L measured eight minutes earlier** — inside `GlucoseTimeline.staleAge` and above the 3.9 target low, so it rendered **fresh and in-range**, with no `LO` token and no warning colour, during a genuine hypo. The next measurement (3.7) was already available from LibreLinkUp; the app had not asked. This was not a display defect — the staleness ladder behaved as specified, and nothing in the render layer can distinguish a reading's *age*, which is knowable, from its *obsolescence*, which is not. Only fetching sooner helps. The premise of the fix is that requests are a budget and staleness is not uniformly harmful: at 7 mmol/L and flat a 15-minute-old reading costs nothing, while approaching 4 it is the difference between a number that describes the user and one that does not. Because glucose is unremarkable the large majority of the time, spending the budget only in the second case leaves the long-run average request rate near today's, so the ban exposure is a burst during a hypo rather than a permanent tripling — which is what makes this acceptable where a uniform 5-minute poll was not (rejected: it triples the *sustained* rate against a service that has banned accounts at ~3 minutes, and a ban costs the entire data stream). The threshold sits at 5.0 rather than the 3.9 band edge deliberately, since tightening only *after* arriving at a low reproduces the failure — the poll is what makes the display late in the first place; the fall-rate trigger covers what the level test cannot, a 9.0 falling hard being comfortable now and not in twenty minutes. `TrendsMath.glucoseRate` is reused rather than a second rate calculation written, so "falling fast" means the same thing to the scheduler as to the arrow on screen. The urgent interval is 5 minutes and never 3, with a test asserting that floor because the constant governs behaviour against a third party that can revoke access outright. `currentPollInterval` is read *before* the loop sleeps so the fetch that just landed governs the wait that follows it, and it starts at the baseline so a first fetch that fails cannot leave the loop spinning at the urgent rate. The background-fetch `earliestBeginDate` is deliberately unchanged at 15 minutes: iOS does not honour it as a schedule, so tightening it would add vendor exposure without adding freshness. Nine XCTest cases pin every branch — baseline on no readings and on flat glucose, the strict `<` threshold boundary in both directions, a fast fall from a comfortable 9.0, a gentle fall staying at baseline, rising-fast staying at baseline, order independence, and the rate-limit floor (XCTest 515 → 524 executed, 5 skipped, 0 failures; swift-testing 204 unchanged). Recorded as `cgm-connect` Decision 12 and Req 3.2a. **This does not make MeData safe to rely on for hypo detection**: the Abbott app alarms from a direct BLE link to the sensor while MeData reads a cloud follower API, so five minutes is better than fifteen and still not real time.

- **Glucose widget sat a reading behind whenever a CGM row was timestamped ahead of the device clock** (`App/GlucoseWidgetPublisher.swift`): `currentSnapshot` queried the closed window `[now − 24 h, now]`, so any `bsl` row stamped even a second into the future was excluded from the read entirely and the snapshot kept the previous reading for as long as the skew lasted. The bug is invisible from the render side, because `GlucoseTimeline.render` already handles exactly this case — it clamps a future reading's age to zero on the stated grounds that "a reading timestamped in the future is clock skew, not a prediction" (Req 5.5) — but with the query's upper bound at `now` that clamp was unreachable: the row never got as far as the snapshot. The asymmetry is what made it hard to see from the app, since the Trends screen queries to the end of the calendar day and so displayed the same row perfectly well. The upper bound is now `now + futureSkewAllowance`, one hour. Bounded rather than open-ended deliberately: a future reading clamps to age zero and therefore renders as *fresh*, so an unbounded window would let a single corrupt far-future row pin the widget to a wrong value indefinitely — an hour absorbs real clock and timezone-rounding skew while capping that blast radius. `TrendsMath.glucoseRate` still filters `timestamp <= now` and so excludes a future reading from the least-squares fit; that is left as-is, since it anchors trend and staleness to the same clock by design, and the effect under skew is a conservative arrow (or none) beside a correct value rather than a wrong one. Every interpolation is `privacy: .public` — `os_log` redacts non-literals by default and would render the timestamps as `<private>`, which is precisely the information these lines exist to carry. `publish.futureReading` logs the observed skew so the field diagnosis can be confirmed or refuted on the next device pass, and `publisher.streamEnded` is an `error`-level line after the `for await` loop, reachable only if the store's broadcaster finishes the stream — a state in which the widget has silently stopped updating for the rest of the process lifetime and which previously produced no signal at all.

- **Device-log flood evicted the plane-fit diagnostics — every "no flat surface" investigation was blind** (2026-07-06): a matte-table capture retest showed the failure persists ("again"), but the collected log for the refusal never contained `event=supportplane.end success=false` (the counters that explain it) or `event=launch buildStamp=…` (which build ran) — only `segmenter.mask` lines in a 6-second window. Root cause: the pre-shutter live segmenter emits `event=segmenter.mask` at `Logger.info` ~2–3.5×/second continuously (in `CoreMLSegmenter.segment()`), flooding the persisted unified-log store and evicting the low-frequency `.info` events before `make logs-device` collects them. Fixed by a per-instance `CoreMLSegmenter.MaskLogCadence`: the pre-shutter (`.livePreview`) path logs at `.debug` (in-memory tier, cannot evict the persisted store), while the one-shot shutter-time capture keeps `.info`. Also added `residual_mm=` (new `LiDARPlaneFitter.debugLastResidualMm`) to the `supportplane.end` failure trace so a residual-too-high refusal is distinguishable from point-starvation/degeneracy. Observability only — no estimation behaviour changed. This is the meta-fix that unblocks root-causing the matte-table refusal: the plane fit was deliberately NOT patched a third time (the gravity/confidence/empty-mask hypotheses don't survive analysis of the current evidence, and the deciding counters are exactly what the flood evicted). Acceptance gate: redeploy (this build + `06b86fa`) → capture on the matte table → `make logs-device` now shows `launch` + `supportplane.end` counters → the plane-fit fix follows precisely next round. - **"No flat surface" on a matte table — LiDAR plane fit required HIGH-only confidence** (2026-07-06): single-view capture refused on matte / low-reflectance tables even after the gravity fix (`09aab63`) resolved it on glossy ones. `LiDARPlaneFitter` seeds RANSAC only from table pixels clearing `τ_conf = 0.66`, which — against ARKit's `ARConfidenceLevel.{low,medium,high}` → `{0,127,255}` mapping — admits **only HIGH (255)**. A matte surface returns a weaker LiDAR signal dominated by **MEDIUM (127 = 0.498)**, so every candidate point was filtered out → `noLidarPoints` → "no flat surface". Fixed by lowering `τ_conf` to `0.40` (Decision 49): MEDIUM-or-better is now accepted and only genuine LOW/zero returns are dropped; the RANSAC 5 mm inlier band + 20 mm residual gate still reject a bad plane and `σ_plane` carries the extra noise (consistent with Decision 46). Regression tests pin both bounds (medium fits, low still refuses). XCTest 383 (3 skipped, 0 failures), swift-testing 114, build + spell green; on-device matte-table retest is the acceptance gate.
